# Character card

For a full new card record stable ID, display name, status (confirmed/proposal), and these ten fields. Use actions and circumstances, not unexplained psychological labels. Preserve author-only facts separately from what the player knows.

1. Situation: livelihood, resources, limitations, current obligations.
2. Want: a specific result and the reason action is needed now.
3. Cost: what she will spend, sacrifice, or risk; what she will not.
4. Boundary: normal method, refusal, and the particular trigger that can change it.
5. Self-understanding: what she believes about her conduct; the observable blind spot.
6. Relationships: reciprocal needs, leverage, and when/how people meet.
7. Knowledge: known facts, mistakes, secrets, and the event allowing each new discovery.
8. Voice: how she asks, refuses, lies, argues and avoids a topic; short concrete examples.
9. Playable presence: actions, spaces, resources or consequences through which players learn about her.
10. Change: observable trigger and resulting decision, not a generic redemption arc.

For competing protagonist motives, test the same three situations for each: accepting work, encountering a target she does not want to kill, receiving a higher competing offer. Compare whether the motive supports recurring action, developing relationships, and a final conflict. Recommend one without treating it as approved.
